**Subject: Technical cooperation for a public-information platform for COP32 — request to meet the Task Force**

Dear [Name],

We were encouraged by the Task Force's stated commitment to reliable, resilient and secure digital services for COP32. Zega Tech PLC [(registration in progress)] has designed a public-information platform (English and Amharic; Android, iOS and web) intended for visitors, residents, journalists and remote followers. We would like to explore cooperation with the Task Force so that our work complements, rather than duplicates, the national digital arrangements.

**Why we are writing to the Task Force**
1. **Alignment:** to learn what digital services are planned, so that we integrate with them or avoid overlap.
2. **Connectivity:** we have designed the platform for low-bandwidth and offline use. We would like to discuss whether discounted or zero-rated access to official information (including for the platform, if it is recognised) is possible, and what to expect of network performance in the venue area.
3. **Hosting and security:** personal data will be stored in Ethiopia (Proclamation No. 1321/2024). We would like to discuss suitable Ethiopian hosting, security requirements, and any assessment or registration processes the Task Force or INSA expect for applications of this kind.
4. **Data and integration:** we propose simple, open, documented interfaces (for programme and alerts) and can work from spreadsheets. We would like to know whether the Task Force has, or plans, a programme-data service, an alert channel or a host-country application.
5. **Emergency communications:** a pathway for authorised safety alerts to appear in the platform, with signing and dual approval, if the Task Force considers this useful.

**What we would like**
- A technical meeting (45 minutes) with the Task Force's focal point.
- Names of the right contacts for hosting/security, data/integration and communications.
- Any technical requirements or standards we should follow.

**What we can share**
A short architecture summary (two data planes: public content on a content-delivery network and personal data in Ethiopian data centres; signed content bundles; offline-first design), our security and privacy approach (OWASP-based verification, independent penetration testing before the pilot and before the event, audit logging, 72-hour incident response), and a demonstration with sample data.

We would be glad to adapt to the Task Force's processes.
